Bring the research into one of your classrooms.

One class. One period. No fee, now or later.

Project Yaadhum is a field research initiative. We are building the first working version of a student transition programme, and we are looking for a small number of schools willing to try it while it is still being shaped.

We do not arrive with a fixed programme, or a fixed answer. We begin with the problem your school is seeing

Four classes. Four different transition questions.

Across Classes 9 to 12, where do your students or parents struggle most with what comes next? We do not ask a school to fit our programme. We shape the first pilot around the problem it is already living with.

Class 9

Explore before choices narrow

What students have been exposed to, what they are curious about, and what deserves exploring before the major decisions begin.

Class 10

Choose with better information

How stream and pathway decisions are actually being made, what the family knows, what is influencing it, and what is missing.

Class 11

Adjust and stay on track

Who is struggling with the transition, what sits behind a drop in performance or confidence, and where support may matter.

Class 12

Navigate exams, courses and colleges

Whether students know the pathways open to them, the examinations and eligibility involved, and what they need to do next.

A school knows the child. The decision does not.

Every school we have sat in already knows a great deal about its students. The difficulty is not effort or care. It is that what a school knows and what a family decides never meet, because they happen at different moments.

What we keep noticing

The decision arrives before the information does

Families choose in a matter of weeks. The things that would have helped them choose well often reach them months later, or not at all.

What a teacher sees has nowhere to go

Something is noticed in September. There is no place to put it, so by the time the stream form appears it has quietly gone.

Nobody goes back to check

Once a choice is made it is treated as settled. Whether it turned out to fit the student is a question almost nobody returns to.

What we would like to understand with you.

Five questions we cannot answer from outside a school. Everything we would do with you is in service of these, and nothing beyond them.

The questions

  • Who actually decides, and who is in the room when it happens
  • What information reaches a family, through whom, and how early
  • What a school already knows that never reaches the decision
  • What it costs a student when the fit turns out to be wrong
  • Whether anything anyone noticed was ever acted on

And what this is not

  • Nothing is being sold to your school. There is no fee, now or later
  • No career score, percentage or prediction about a child
  • No ranking of students against each other
  • No diagnosis, and no permanent labels
  • No claim that one exercise can settle a student’s future

In practice this is one class, about one period, and an honest conversation afterwards about what we both learned. Parents are informed and consent obtained before any minor takes part, and anything we publish is anonymised. A student should leave with more possibilities to explore, not another label attached to them.

Small enough to learn properly.

Six steps, start to finish. The first pilots are design partnerships rather than finished products, which is exactly why they are free.

What a school gives us

  • One section, around 30 to 50 students
  • About 45 minutes of one period
  • One coordinator we can work with
  • The permissions the pilot requires
  • An honest conversation afterwards
  1. 01

    Choose one problem

    A short conversation with the school to identify one transition question worth investigating.

  2. 02

    Choose one cohort

    Usually one section, around 30 to 50 students.

  3. 03

    Run one focused session

    Around 45 minutes of student time, adapted to the question we chose together. It is not an aptitude test.

  4. 04

    Make sense of what we find

    We look for patterns, exceptions, and information that deserves someone’s attention.

  5. 05

    Share it back

    The school receives a cohort summary and the agreed findings, with a clear read of what appears to matter.

  6. 06

    Recheck where useful

    A few weeks later, where the design calls for it, we revisit what was flagged and tell you what changed.

Written as a promise to a parent, not as terms and conditions.

For a school pilot, the agreed individual findings go to the school. We do not distribute individual reports to volunteers, sponsors, other schools or the public.

Collect only what we need

Every field a programme asks for has to have a reason. We do not collect student information simply because we can.

Parents know beforehand

The programme, what is collected and how it will be used are explained before a minor takes part, and consent is obtained where required.

No ranking, no labels

A student’s responses are never used to rank them against others or to predict what they will become.

Research is anonymised

Anything used for research or publication is anonymised unless separate, explicit permission has been given.

Why are the first pilots free? Because we are still learning too.

No fee, no obligation

Project Yaadhum is studying how students in Tier 2 and Tier 3 communities actually navigate school transitions. We would rather learn from real schools than build around assumptions.

The first pilots are therefore free, with no obligation to purchase anything afterwards. If this ever becomes a paid school programme, we will say so plainly rather than quietly.
